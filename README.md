# Gernreich (Michael's Ideas)

Projects by Michael Carnright, each with its own page.

## LaserMadeMusic

Musical instruments cut from 3 mm Baltic birch plywood on a laser, and the files to
build them. Trumpets, flutes, ocarinas, panpipes, bagpipe chanters, a lyre-harp, a
kalimba and more, with the generators and tools behind them. The cutting and the playing
are on the [LaserMadeMusic channel](https://www.youtube.com/@LaserMadeMusic).
**[Open it](lasermademusic.html)**

## Hexagonal dice box

A finger-jointed hexagonal box in 3 mm ply with seven hexagonal pockets from glued insert layers, for a polyhedral dice set, and a lid held by 2 × 1 mm magnets. **[Open it](https://gernreich.github.io/hex-dice-box/)**

## Win(d)Bag

A bagpipe tune player begun in BASIC on an IBM PCjr and written in Visual Basic 3 between 1995 and 2000, with its source and a web edition that plays the tunes in the browser. **[Open it](https://gernreich.github.io/windbag/)**

## Gernreich writing

A script in which every letter is two steps on a square grid and every word is one
unbroken line, with a live writer and reader, and Article 1 of the Universal Declaration
of Human Rights drawn as a single line. **[Open it](https://gernreich.github.io/gernreich-writing/)**

## Coprime intervals

Coprime pairs as a grid of rays, as Turk's head knots and as the musical intervals inside one octave. **[Open it](https://gernreich.github.io/coprime-intervals/)**

## Harmonics

Michael's 1992 search for a consonance rating for musical intervals: BASIC programs
written on a second-hand IBM PCjr, then Fortran and C, that count how often each ratio
turns up among pairs of whole numbers. The scans, exact transcriptions and what each
program found; the starting point of Coprime intervals. **[Open it](https://gernreich.github.io/harmonics/)**

## Lapping

Two runners on a track: the faster laps the slower every Z = XY/(X − Y), the equation
Michael worked out at sixteen. The same equation gives when clock hands meet, the month
of the Moon's phases and how often the Earth overtakes Mars. **[Open it](https://gernreich.github.io/lapping/)**

## Atomic layer memory

Data stored as the stacking order of close-packed layers of silicon or carbon atoms,
with a spacer layer between every bit and an atomic force microscope tip reading it from
the side. Michael thought it up after reading Drexler's *Nanosystems*. **[Open it](https://gernreich.github.io/atomic-layer-memory/)**

## Knot crossings

Two ropes crossing each other 8 times, and all 256 ways to choose over or under, drawn
by hand and numbered in binary. The scans, the index of knots Michael recognised, and
every drawing worked out again and checked: square knot, sheet bend, Carrick bend and
their relatives. Each drawing is also closed into a loop and named as a knot or link
by its Jones and Alexander polynomials (the Carrick bend closes to the Carrick mat), and
every knotted one carries its number in *The Ashley Book of Knots*, matched through
Eric's (NautiKnots) enumeration on the IGKT forum. Any drawing can also be turned in
3D as two ropes, loose or joined, plain or laid. **[Open it](https://gernreich.github.io/knot-crossings/)**
· **[Turn it in 3D](https://gernreich.github.io/knot-crossings/turn.html)**

## Sixteen boolean functions

Every two-input logic function, with its expressions enumerated, the weights a small neural network uses for each, and every function applied to every pair. **[Open it](https://gernreich.github.io/Logic-Research/)**

## Gray and de Bruijn

Gray codes and de Bruijn sequences walked through three alphabets from these projects:
the atomic-layer-memory stacks, Zellweger's Logic Alphabet and the Gernreich writing
grid. A ruler that reads its own position through a nine-cell window, two encoder discs,
and two rings: every truth table across from its negation, and the 28 grid letters with
the least turning possible. Each comes as a laser cut file, and there are 3D views of the
stacks and of the ring on a four-dimensional cube. Proposed, worked out and built by
[Claude](https://www.anthropic.com/claude) at Michael's request. **[Open it](https://gernreich.github.io/gray-and-de-bruijn/)**

## Best bang for the buck

Which folders to clean up first on a full drive, and where to stop: the running-total curve and its point nearest the origin. **[Open it](https://gernreich.github.io/bang-for-the-buck/)**
